Something That's Bothering Me Right Now

RantFinanceWarning

FDIC insured. Four words that are supposed to mean your money is safe. Right now they mean absolutely nothing, and it's doing my head in.

Remember 2023?

March 2023. Silicon Valley Bank went down after punters pulled $42 billion out in a single day. Third biggest bank failure in American history. Signature Bank went days later, First Republic a few weeks after that - three of the four largest bank collapses ever, inside two months.

Bank stocks got smashed. First Republic dropped 52% in a morning. And the whole country suddenly gave a toss about one thing: is my money FDIC insured?

Fair enough too. 88% of SVB's deposits were sitting above the $250,000 insured limit. People got a very fast, very public lesson in what happens when you're over the line.

From that moment on, everyone checked for the FDIC badge. And every fintech in the world noticed.

So they all slapped it on

Suddenly every app had 'FDIC insured' plastered across the landing page. Nice interface, 5% interest, big green tick, safe as houses.

Except most of them were never banks. They were apps sitting on top of a middleman called Synapse, which sat on top of a bank you'd never heard of. Three layers, and you were dealing with the layer that had no licence.

In April 2024 Synapse went bankrupt. Not the bank. The middleman. And that's when everyone found out what they'd actually bought.

85,000 people locked out. $112 million of customer money missing. The FDIC couldn't do a thing about any of it, because FDIC insurance pays out when a bank fails. No bank failed. The plumbing failed. Not covered. Never was.

So we all learned to look for the badge, and then the badge stopped meaning anything. That's what's bothering me.

And the influencers

These apps didn't blow up on their own. They got shoved down everyone's throat by influencer cunts recommending shit products for affiliate money.

Same script every time - 'better than a bank', 'FDIC insured', 'why is nobody talking about this'. Nobody read the fine print because nobody got paid to read the fine print. They got paid per signup.

Then it collapsed, people lost their savings, and those same accounts rolled straight onto the next sponsored product without a single follow-up video. California fined Yotta a million dollars for deceptive practices - for marketing itself as safe and claiming accounts were FDIC insured. A million. Against $112 million of other people's money. Cost of doing business.

If someone's paid per signup, they're not giving you advice. They're a salesman with a ring light.

My own near miss

I opened a Go2Bank account a while back. Never put a cent in, and thank god.

The whole thing felt off from the first screen. What finished it was trying to contact them - there was nobody to ring. Chat only. That's it. I'm about to hand over money and there isn't one human on the end of a phone.

I walked. Any outfit that wants your deposits but won't give you a phone number has already told you what you're worth to them.

How to not get done

Name the actual bankThe app is not the bank. Find the 'banking services provided by [bank], Member FDIC' line
Check it's realSearch that bank on the FDIC's BankFind. Not there? Neither is your money
Try to phone themChat-only support on something holding your savings is a red flag, not a feature
Follow the moneyIf the recommendation comes with a signup link, it's an ad. Treat it like one

Where I sit on it

I'm not anti-fintech. I use plenty of it and I'll chase a bank bonus all day - there's a stack of them on this site right now.

The difference is I know whose balance sheet my money is actually sitting on. A flashy rate means nothing if the outfit between you and the bank folds and takes the ledger with it.

And when a deal goes up here it's because I've looked at it, not because someone paid me to.

Bottom line: if you can't name the bank holding your money, you haven't got a bank account. You've got an app and a hope.
