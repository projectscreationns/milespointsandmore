Something That's Bothering Me Right Now

RantFinanceWarning

I watched a video this week about how millions of people got tricked into using a bank that isn't a bank. It's been in my head ever since, because I nearly walked into one myself.

Here's the short version. A pile of slick fintech apps marketed themselves like banks. Nice app, high interest rate, 'FDIC insured' plastered everywhere. They were never banks. They were apps sitting on top of a middleman called Synapse, which sat on top of an actual bank.

In April 2024 Synapse went bankrupt. Not the bank - the middleman. And that's when everyone found out what they'd actually signed up for.

85,000 people locked out. $112 million of customer money missing. And the FDIC couldn't help a single one of them, because FDIC insurance covers you when a bank fails. No bank failed here. The app's plumbing failed. That's not covered, and it never was.

The part that actually winds me up

These apps didn't go viral on their own. They got pushed. Hard. By finance influencers who took the affiliate money and read the script.

Every one of them ran the same lines - 'better than a bank', 'FDIC insured', 'why is nobody talking about this'. Nobody read the fine print because nobody was paid to read the fine print. They were paid per signup.

Then the thing collapsed, people lost their savings, and those same accounts moved on to the next sponsored product without so much as a follow-up video. California eventually fined Yotta $1 million for deceptive practices - for marketing itself as safe and claiming accounts were FDIC insured. A million dollars. Against $112 million of other people's money.

If someone's getting paid per signup, they're not your advisor. They're a salesperson with a camera.

My near miss

I opened a Go2Bank account a while back. Never funded it - and I'm glad, because the whole thing felt off from the first screen.

What sealed it was trying to contact them. There was no one to call. Chat only. That's it. I'm about to hand money to an outfit and there is not a single human being on the end of a phone line.

That was enough for me. I walked. Call it a gut feeling, but a business that wants your deposits and won't give you a phone number has told you exactly how much it values you as a customer.

How to not get caught

Find the real bankThe app isn't the bank. Look for 'Banking services provided by [bank], Member FDIC'
Check it existsSearch that bank name on the FDIC's BankFind. No entry, no money
Try to phone themChat-only support on a place holding your savings is a red flag, not a feature
Ask who's paidIf a recommendation comes with a signup link, treat it as an ad, because it is

Where I actually sit on this

I'm not telling you to avoid fintech. I use plenty of it. I'll chase a bank bonus all day long and I've written up a stack of them on this site.

The difference is I keep the money at an actual bank, and I know whose balance sheet it's sitting on. A high rate on an app means nothing if the outfit between you and the bank goes under and takes the ledger with it.

And when I put a deal on this site, it's because I've looked at it - not because someone paid me to.

Bottom line: if you can't name the bank holding your money, you don't have a bank account. You have an app, and a hope.
