The Annual Fee Cards I Actually Keep

Credit CardsStrategy
Updated Jul 25, 2026

I hold a pile of cards, but most of them are free. These are the five I actually pay for - and the reason each one survives the cull.

Business cards aren't in here. This is the personal wallet only.

What I pay
$1,775
a year, across five cards
Rule for keeping one
Use it or cut it
credits I'd spend anyway, or it goes

Chase Sapphire Reserve - $795

The big one, and the one I re-check every year. At $795 it only works if you actually run the credits: the $300 travel credit is the easy part, then there's the dining, hotel and event credits stacked on top, plus lounge access and IHG Platinum status.

The catch is they're chopped into monthly and half-yearly chunks, so it rewards being organised. If you're not the type to track that, this is the first card you should cut.

Capital One Venture X - $395

The easiest premium card to justify. $300 travel credit plus 10,000 anniversary miles more or less wipes out the fee before you've done anything clever, and you get lounge access on top.

No mental gymnastics, no monthly credit calendar. Set and forget.

BofA Atmos Rewards Summit - $395

The newest one in the wallet. Alaska's premium card: 8 lounge passes and 8 wifi passes a year, 3x on dining and foreign spend, and no foreign transaction fees.

The lounge passes alone are worth more than the fee if you fly Alaska at all. The Global Companion Award needs $60k of spend, so I treat that as a nice-to-have rather than the reason to hold it.

Chase Hyatt - $95

Free night every year at a Category 1-4 property. That's the entire argument. A decent Hyatt runs well past $95 a night, so the card pays for itself the moment you book.

Hyatt punches above its weight for award nights generally - this is the cheapest way to keep a foot in that program.

Amex Marriott - $95

Same logic. Annual free night up to 35,000 points, which comfortably beats the $95 in most decent Marriotts. Plus the elite nights that come with it, which help top up status without staying anywhere.

What I don't keep

Everything else in my wallet is a $0 card - Discover, Bluecash Everyday, the Amazon card, Citi Diamond, PayPal, a Capital One. No fee means no annual decision to make, so they just sit there earning.

The test is simple: if I wouldn't spend the credits anyway, the card's not earning its fee. Every one of these five clears that. The moment one doesn't, it gets downgraded or cancelled.

None of this is financial advice. Everything here is simply an opinion. Always check current terms with the provider and consult a licensed financial adviser before making any financial decisions.
