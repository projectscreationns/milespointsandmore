The Annual Fee Cards I Actually Keep

Credit CardsStrategy
Updated Jul 25, 2026

I hold a stack of cards but most of them are free. These are the five I actually pay for, and exactly why each one survives.

No business cards here - this is the personal wallet only.

Total fees
$1,775
a year, all five cards
Out of the CSR alone
$5,000+
in four months

Chase Sapphire Reserve - $795

Yes, $795. And I'd do it again tomorrow.

I took it for the 150k bonus and I've been putting it to full use. Four months in and I've already pulled over $5,000 of value out of it - multiple luxury hotel stays, flights, holidays booked, and a business class seat on a 16 hour long haul. That's not theoretical points-nerd maths, that's stuff I've actually flown and slept in.

The fee is a rounding error next to that. Just know the credits come in monthly and half-yearly chunks, so if you're not going to track them, don't bother with this card.

BofA Atmos Summit - $395

This one's about status, not points.

I fly AA a fair bit and all my status miles go to AA, so I can hold my Gold. This card is what helps me keep it ticking over. If you're loyal to one airline, a card that props up your status is worth more than one that throws points at you.

The 8 lounge passes and no foreign transaction fees are a nice bonus on top.

Capital One Venture X - $395

The laziest $395 I spend. $300 travel credit plus 10,000 anniversary miles basically cancels the fee before I've done anything, and lounge access comes along for the ride.

No credit calendar, no hoops. It just quietly pays for itself every year.

Chase Hyatt - $95

Free night every year at a Category 1-4 hotel. That's the whole argument and it's a good one - any decent Hyatt is well past $95 a night, so it's paid for the second I book.

Hyatt is also just a better program than most for award nights. Cheapest way to keep a foot in the door.

Amex Marriott - $95

Same deal. Annual free night up to 35,000 points, which beats $95 at basically any Marriott worth staying in. The elite nights it throws in help top up status without actually staying anywhere.

If you want it: apply through my Amex referral link.

What I don't pay for

Everything else is a $0 card - Discover, Amex Blue Cash Everyday, the Amazon card, Citi Diamond, PayPal, a Capital One. No fee means no annual decision, so they just sit there earning.

The test is simple: would I spend those credits anyway? If yes, the card stays. If I'm hunting for ways to use them, it's already failed - downgrade it or cancel it.

None of this is financial advice. Everything here is simply an opinion. Always check current terms with the provider and consult a licensed financial adviser before making any financial decisions.
